CHAPTER 46

Before Departure

Aether woke before the sky had fully brightened. Whitestone was already beginning to stir. A bell sounded somewhere beyond the roofs; wheels rolled over stone; merchants called to one another as they opened their shops.

When Aether had first entered the city, everything had seemed too large - too many people, too many buildings, too much noise. Somewhere along the way, that had changed. He knew streets now. He recognized shops. He knew which corner would smell of fresh bread in the morning.

Soon he would leave.

This time he was not fleeing, asking for help, or being pushed onward by someone else's decision. He had chosen the road himself.

The thought left him both excited and uneasy.

He tried to imagine what he would have thought of that choice before leaving Gray Vale. Back then, roads had led to places he knew or places other people named for him. Since then, every road had widened the world and complicated it. Whitestone had done that more than any place before it. The city had given Stony Creek help, but it had also shown him how many troubles could exist beyond the reach of a single village.

Aether was not certain he understood any of it better. He only knew he no longer wanted to turn away because the answer might be difficult.

In the courtyard, Kaelan was already checking tack and supplies.

"This early?" Aether asked through a yawn.

Kaelan did not look up. "We have a road ahead of us."

"We're not leaving this minute."

"We will be soon enough."

Aether smiled. That sounded exactly like Kaelan.

Nearby, Edwin sat at a table with several maps and a worn notebook. Aether leaned over his shoulder.

"What's that?"

"A map."

"I know that. I mean the book."

"Old notes."

Aether opened it. The pages were crowded with observations: changes in trade roads, prices, displaced families, conditions in different towns, even weather and travel notes. Some entries were more than a decade old.

"You've been keeping all this?"

"Habit."

"Why?"

Edwin considered the question. "People forget. Paper usually doesn't."

Aether turned another page and saw a familiar name written in younger handwriting: Riverport.

Before he could read further, Edwin closed the book.

"Don't dig through everything."

"I was only looking."

"You'll have time later."

Aether grinned. That answer told him more than Edwin probably intended. The old man truly meant to take them with him.

Later that morning, they went to the defense offices, where a small party was preparing to head north: scouts, hunters, patrolmen, and a supply wagon carrying food and medicine.

Kaelan stood watching for a long time.

An officer checking the roster noticed them. "You're the ones from Stony Creek?"

Kaelan nodded.

"Lord Rayne signed the order himself. We'll go see what you're dealing with."

Kaelan was silent for a moment. "Thank you."

The officer waved it away. "Thank the lord. We're only doing the work."

Kaelan watched until the party had left the square. Only then did he let out a long breath. The danger at home had not vanished, but for the first time since leaving Gray Vale, people beyond the village were moving to help.

Aether stood beside him without speaking. He knew Kaelan well enough not to fill the silence. The riders disappeared one by one into the morning traffic until only the supply wagon remained visible, its canvas cover moving above the crowd.

"You could still go with them," Aether said at last.

Kaelan kept his eyes on the road. "I know."

"Do you want to?"

A long pause followed. "Part of me does." Kaelan finally looked at him. "But going home because help is coming would only answer the question we started with. It wouldn't answer the one we found here."

Aether understood. Stony Creek was still home. That was precisely why Kaelan was choosing not to return yet.

Around midday, the quartermaster came by Edwin's place and took one look at the equipment in the courtyard.

"Of course," he said. "I should have guessed. Rayne calls for you, Riverport is in trouble, and now you're packing."

Edwin glanced up. "You sound disappointed."

"I'm impressed by how predictable you are."

Aether laughed, then asked, "You don't think it's dangerous?"

"Of course it's dangerous," the quartermaster said. "That's why Edwin is going."

His expression grew more serious. "The news is getting worse. Caravans connected to Riverport and Ironbridge are already changing routes. If that continues, cities far from the dispute will feel it too."

Edwin nodded.

Before leaving, the quartermaster looked at the boys. "If you've decided to go, don't treat it like a story. Watch. Listen. Don't rush to decide what something means."

Aether nodded carefully. Kaelan did the same.

That afternoon, Aether went out alone. He did not tell anyone where he was going. He simply walked.

He passed the central square, the granaries, the taverns, and the street where he and Kaelan had first stood staring at Whitestone. The market was still loud. Porters still hurried between warehouses. Patrols still moved through the streets. An old fruit seller called out his prices. Children ran past him laughing. An argument outside a tavern ended, moments later, in laughter.

The city continued to live.

That, more than anything, stayed with him. Whitestone had not become a symbol or a lesson simply because he had learned something here. Bakers still needed to bake, guards still needed to change watch, children still chased one another between carts, and people still complained about prices. The city was made of lives before it was made of walls, offices, or maps.

Aether thought of the unnamed patch on Edwin's map. Perhaps every place looked small from far enough away. It did not mean the people inside it were small.

But Aether no longer saw it in quite the same way.

Months ago, he might only have thought: What a great city.

Now other questions came first. Why were more people leaving their homes? Why was work becoming harder to find? Why were cities that once depended on one another beginning to quarrel? Why were the beasts changing? Why did people who had once been friends begin to treat one another as threats?

He stopped in the middle of the street and realized, with some amusement, what was happening to him.

He was becoming the sort of person who always asked why.

Aether laughed to himself and kept walking.

By evening, Kaelan was arranging the last supplies. Edwin sat in the courtyard sharpening a blade, the setting sun flashing along its edge.

Aether sat beside him.

"What happened in Riverport?"

Edwin kept drawing the stone along the blade.

For a while, the only sound was the faint scrape of metal.

"It was the first time I learned," he said at last, "that not every enemy is human."

Aether sat straighter. "What does that mean?"

Edwin shook his head. "Not every story should be told before its time."

He sheathed the blade. The conversation was over.

Later, Kaelan sat in the courtyard looking at the prepared horses.

"Why did you agree to take us?" he asked.

Edwin checked a length of rope. "I didn't agree."

Kaelan looked at him.

Aether, sitting nearby, laughed. "You didn't say no either."

Edwin gave him a look. "You talk too much."

Aether's grin only widened.

After a moment, Edwin's expression softened. "Harold sent you this far because he believed you needed to see more than Gray Vale. I suppose I can at least make sure you don't get yourselves killed while doing it."

Kaelan lowered his eyes. "My father knew you would come?"

"Harold knows me well enough to be annoying."

The next morning, they were ready before sunrise.

Whitestone's gates opened to the first movement of carts and travelers. Behind them, the city was waking as it always did. Ahead, the southern road ran toward country Aether had never seen.

He looked back once at the walls.

When he had arrived, Whitestone had seemed like the largest thing in the world. Now he understood that even a city like this could be affected by a road failing far away, by a village emptying, by grain arriving late, by fear passing from one place to another.

The world had not become smaller.

It had become connected.

Aether turned south.

Riverport waited somewhere beyond the road. So did Edwin's memories, the question Rayne could not answer, and whatever had begun to move beneath all the smaller troubles they had seen.

For the first time, Aether was not simply following the road because it was the only one left to him.

He had chosen it.

Kaelan rode a little ahead, quiet as ever. Edwin kept an even pace beside him, his attention already on the road. Aether looked from one to the other and then toward the country opening before them. None of them knew exactly what waited at Riverport. That uncertainty no longer felt like emptiness. It felt like a question they had decided to carry together.
