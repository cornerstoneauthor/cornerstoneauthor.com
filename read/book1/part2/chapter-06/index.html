CHAPTER 6

The Fork in the Road

When winter truly descended upon Tide Bay, the sea fell into a stranger, deeper silence than in years past. The morning mists sank heavy and lingered late into the day, shrouding the distant silhouettes of ships that seemed to drift like shadows. The shoals of fish did not return. In the beginning, the townsfolk would gather along the timbered piers to debate the reason, but in time, the whispers failed. For all who dwelt there knew that something held no heed for the talk of men. Months had elapsed since Alvin’s departure. Tide Bay remained in peace, yet it had lost that certainty which once anchored it. Some packed their possessions and departed; others remained behind, each soul choosing a path across the fracturing world. Seren found her days consumed by ever-growing burdens. Much of the labor that had once belonged to her mother slipped unbidden to her own shoulders. She rose before the sun took the sky and lay down long after it had sunk beneath the deep. Maturity did not claim her with trumpets or grand announcements; rather, it came in a thousand quiet obligations, slowly pressing the weight of duty into the hollow of her palm. One evening, returning to her home at dusk, she found her mother seated by the window, mending a network of hemp nets. The dying sunlight spilling across the plain oak table caught the strands of silver reclaiming her mother’s hair. As she worked, sleep overtook her, leaving the bone needle still clutched in her calloused fingers. Seren stood there in the shadows for a long time. She did not wake her mother. Instead, she stepped forward, gently took the weaving from her hands, and silently mended what remained. That night, sleep would not come to her. She realized then, with a cold and grave certainty, that time does not merely reforge oneself—it decays and alters all that one holds dear.

A few days later, messengers from the northern haven arrived once more. The rumor ran through Tide Bay like wildfire. They brought offers of rich prosperity and renewed their summons to Matthew, begging him to leave this forgotten coast. Many were certain that this time, he would accept. At twilight, Seren found Matthew down by the water’s edge. The sea wind howled across the shore, scattering wood shavings at his feet. He sat beside an ancient hull, fitting new planks to old timbers, his face calm as ever.

"They have come for you again."

"Aye."

"The terms they offer are generous."

"Aye. They are."

"Then why remain?"

Matthew fell silent, looking out over the dark rolling tide for a long space.

"When I was a boy," he said softly,

"I desired nothing more than to fly beyond these shores."

"And later?"

"Later, as a youth, I still wished to go."

"And now?"

"Now, I don't know whether the world beyond is truly greater."

He raised a hand, gesturing toward the distant horizon.

"But I do know that there are tasks that require someone to stand fast."

He pointed to the ships resting upon the sands.

"These vessels require timber and oar."

He pointed to the smoke rising from the hamlet.

"My kin require a hand to sustain them."

Finally, he turned his gaze back to the endless deep.

"Some men are born for the far reaches of the earth. Others are born to guard the hearth."

"And which of those are you?" Matthew offered a faint, weary smile.

"I don't know. Yet for now, this is the road I have chosen to walk."

On her return journey, Matthew’s words echoed tirelessly in Seren’s mind. This is the road I have chosen to walk. The winter deepened, bitter and unyielding. The haul from the nets remained scant. Yet another household gathered their belongings and abandoned Tide Bay. At their departure, no tears were shed. The neighbors simply assembled in solemn silence to heave their crates, standing upon the wharf to wave farewell as the saltwater widened between them. The vessel faded into the mists. Seren watched from the rear of the crowd. An epiphany struck her: every soul upon this earth was marching along its own solitary path.

Some would depart. Some would remain. Some would seek new realms beyond the seas. Some would stand guard over what was ancient and known. And what of herself? She had never truly met the question face to face. That night, the dream returned once more. A quiet twilight stretched out between heaven and earth. The wind swelled through great plains of high grass, and in the distance, the distant city stood silent, untouched by time. Throughout the years, this vision had never fully forsaken her. As a child, she had feared it. Later, she had grown accustomed to its presence. Now, she waited for it. She walked forward along that familiar pathway. The road was clearer now than ever before. The wind whispered through the tall blades of grass, creating a soft, tiresome murmur. She did not know how long she walked. Until before her came a divide. Two paths diverged, stretching out toward separate destinations. Equally wide. Equally silent. Equally boundless and unknowable at their ends.

Seren halted her steps. For the first time within the realm of dreams, she was met with a choice. No voice came to guide her tongue. No guardian appeared in the mist. No signs were engraved upon the stone. There was only the wind. There were only the paths. As she stood captive to the silence, Matthew’s words resounded anew within her heart: "this is the road I have chosen to walk." She thought of those who had departed, and of those who remained in the cold; she thought of her mother, and of the lonely winter overtaking Tide Bay. And with that, she stride forward, setting her foot upon one of the severed paths. At the very instant her heel struck the earth, a profound and unthinkable calm washed over her soul. Not because she knew what lay at the end—but because she had found the will to march forward. The realm of dreams slowly dissolved into the void. She awoke into the pitch black. Night still reigned outside her window. The rhythmic tide beat against the cliffs, dull and constant.

Seren sat on the edge of her bed, gazing out into the darkness. An understanding awoke within her: sometimes, a person does not step forward because they can see the destination. Sometimes the way ahead only begins to reveal itself after the first step is taken. When morning came, she walked alone toward the harbor. The coastal dirt path split in two before her. She paused for only a moment. Then she took one path and continued onward. The wind blew in from the boundless reaches of the sea. A flock of seabirds rose into the wind, climbing toward the open sky. She still did not know what future awaited her. She did not know what the ancient citadel demanded of her soul, nor where this path would finally end. Yet for the very first time, she felt no need to demand all answers at once. The dream had never promised her a destination. It had only shown her, time and again, the next step. And she was learning, at last, how to answer.

When deep winter descended upon Tide Bay, the sea seemed to lose all color. The sky remained a desolate grey-white, and the wind blew from far out at sea, carrying a frost that bit to the bone. Fewer souls walked the docks now, and fewer vessels dared the waves. The shoals had still not returned; though now and again a ship would limp home

❦

A few days later, the sky unexpectedly cleared. Seren set out to bring some dried fish to old Eden. The old man dwelt in a weathered timber cottage at the village edge, where piles of timber lay stacked before the door. As was his habit, he sat outside, whittling a piece of wood. They spoke for a long time. As she prepared to take her leave, old Eden opened a worn wooden chest and withdrew a carving—a seabird wrought from wood.

"Take it," he said. Seren paused, taken aback.

"For me?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

The old man fell silent for a long moment, his gaze drifting toward the boundless sea.

"I do not know," he murmured.

"I simply felt... perhaps you have greater need of it than I. Who can say when everything might suddenly change?"

Seren accepted the carving and tucked it away. At dusk, she sat alone upon the crag where she had plyesd as a child, watching the sun sink into the waves. Memories flooded back—of her mother, of old Eden, and of the countless days of her upbringing. A thought, one she had never dared entertain before, quietly surfaced: What if, one day, I were to leave this place?

❦

Night fell, and with it, the dream returned. Twilight hung suspended between heaven and earth. The wind rustled through the tall grass, and in the distance, the ancient city stood silent and proud. Seren walked the path before her, following it as it narrowed, until at last a slender stone archway barred the way. Reaching the threshold, she looked down and discovered the wooden seabird in her hands—identical in every mark to the one old Eden had bestowed upon her that day. She stepped forward to pass through the archway, but found she could not carry it across. Not because it was too heavy.

Nor because it was too grand. It was as though the road ahead simply had no space to hold both the token and her journey. She stood frozen before the gate for a long time. The wind swept through from the other side of the stone arch, and the road beyond stretched onward into the unknown. There was no voice. No command. No explanation. Only a quiet understanding that took root within her: If I cling to this, I can go no further. At last, with gentle hands, she laid the wooden bird down before the stone threshold, and stepped through alone. Beyond the gate, the road continued, winding toward distant horizons. And though nothing else had changed, she knew she had crossed a threshold within herself as well.

❦

The dream ebbed slowly away. When she opened her eyes, dawn had not yet broken. The rhythm of waves crashing against the shore echoed from afar. Seren sat at the edge of her bed, motionless for a long while. At last, she truly understood. To grow up is not merely to learn how to choose; sometimes, it is learning how to let go. Not for a lack of love, but because one treasures it all the more. When morning came, she placed the woodcarving upon the window sill. Sunlight bathed the bird’s carved wings. She watched it silently for a moment, then turned and stepped forward into the new day. And the road ahead lay waiting.
