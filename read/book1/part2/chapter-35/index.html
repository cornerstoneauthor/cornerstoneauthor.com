CHAPTER 35

The Light

← PreviousPart II Contents

Toward evening the road climbed through the last stand of scattered trees and brought the three travelers to an open ridge. Hills fell away in long folds beneath them. Several rivers reflected the slanting sun, and farther off the pale surface of distant water seemed to merge with the sky. The wind carried the scents of soil and river water, with a faint trace of salt that none of them could quite place. Kael stopped to study the road toward Riverport and told his companions that they had at last come close to the city. For him, that meant finding trustworthy lodgings and learning the rules of the gates and ferries. For Elowen, it meant that the life awaiting her beyond the Order's walls was about to begin in earnest.

Seren stood on the ridge without speaking. She remembered leaving the fishing village, uncertain of what lay ahead; the dreams and summons no one had been able to explain; and everything she had experienced in Rivergate. For much of the journey she had treated the Echo as a lantern in the darkness. When she felt it, she knew where to place her next step. When it fell silent, she feared she had strayed from the path. Now the familiar stillness had returned, but she no longer wanted to demand directions from it. She looked out across the broad country and, for the first time, allowed herself to remain in the present without asking what the next moment would bring.

At first nothing around her changed. Wind moved through the grass, birds circled above the riverbanks, and Kael was speaking quietly with Elowen about whether they should travel farther before nightfall. Then Seren became aware that the sounds seemed to be receding. She could still hear them, yet a stillness deeper than hearing was unfolding within her, making the world appear both extraordinarily clear and strangely distant. She was not afraid. She felt as though she had reached an unseen threshold. Nothing commanded her to cross it, but she knew that the call she had followed for so long had never been an accident.

It was as though a veil, finer than anything woven by human hands, had lifted before her. Seren saw the light. It was not the setting sun, nor flame, nor lightning. It did not force her to shut her eyes. Instead, she understood for the first time that every bright thing she had ever seen was only a distant reflection of it. She could not say where it came from, nor describe its boundaries in shape or color. She knew only that she was surrounded by a reality beyond anything she could measure. It did not oppress her or make her feel too insignificant to approach. In its presence, she no longer needed to prove that she was worthy of being received.

She had once believed that she must make every choice correctly if she wished to hear the Echo again. She had feared that hesitation at a crossroads meant disloyalty, and that terror before the wolves meant her faith had failed. None of these judgments was answered point by point. No voice offered an explanation.

Yet they dissolved like mist in the light. She understood that what she had followed was never merely a feeling she could summon when she needed comfort. The Echo had never been the destination. All along, it had been leading her toward this light, a presence that had existed long before she knew how to recognize it.

She remembered the small moments along the way: someone reading wagon tracks when she could not choose a road, someone speaking of the sun hidden by clouds, someone standing between her and the claws of a wolf, someone handing her water when she was wounded or weary. She had thought of these as fortunate kindnesses encountered during a difficult journey. Now she saw them differently. Her companions were not substitutes for the light, nor steps she had needed to climb in order to reach it. They were lives traveling beside her. In learning to accept their help, she had also been learning that she had never been led alone.

Tears slipped down her cheeks. They came neither from sorrow nor simply from joy, but from emotions for which she had never found names, now given somewhere to rest. She felt known, seen, and patiently awaited. Even during the hours when she had been most afraid of losing her way, this reality had not withdrawn from her. She could not tell how long she stood in the light. Perhaps it was only a moment; perhaps it held the weight of a journey much longer than her own. She heard no command and received no complete account of the future. Yet she understood that some encounters mattered not because they told a person what to do, but because they revealed where that person belonged.

Kael, standing a little way off, saw no light. Before him were the same hills in the evening wind, the darkening sky, and Seren standing motionless on the ridge. Yet a peace he could not explain came over him, and he found himself releasing the sword hilt on which his hand so often rested. His shoulder still ached. The dangers of the road had not vanished. But for a moment he no longer felt compelled to prove, through constant readiness for battle, that he could protect everyone. For years he had trusted vigilance above all else, as though relaxing his guard would invite loss. He still did not know where this peace came from. For the first time, however, he was willing to admit that some things in the world might be real without needing his sword to preserve them.

Elowen did not see what Seren saw either. She noticed Seren's tears first, and then the extraordinary calm with which they fell. She thought of the testimonies copied and carefully preserved in the library of the Order of the Sacred Lamp, and of the elders who discussed them with both reverence and restraint. She had believed those accounts worthy of preservation, but had never dared to assume that she would come close to the reality toward which they pointed. Now a profound wonder stirred within her, as though the years separating her from those written witnesses had suddenly drawn near.

“So it was true,” she whispered. The wind nearly carried her words away. She had not spoken them for anyone else to hear. At last she understood why the elderly sister had so often said that those who truly experienced such things were seldom eager to speak. It was not because they wished to keep a secret for themselves. Words could simply be too small for certain moments. Elowen did not kneel or attempt to explain what was happening to Seren. She stood where she was and let her tears fall. Leaving the Order, she now knew, need not mean turning away from her former life. Perhaps everything she had learned there was only beginning to reveal its meaning.

The wind continued across the ridge. When Seren opened her eyes, the world had not become another place. The rivers still flowed eastward, the shadows of the trees lengthened beneath the sinking sun, Kael stood ahead, and Elowen was beside her. The light had left nothing she could hold in her hands. It had not removed all uncertainty from the years to come. But she knew she would no longer regard every silence of the Echo as proof that she had been forsaken. She had not been searching for a direction in which she could never make a mistake. She had been searching for a belonging that did not vanish when she was weak. The Echo had not been leading her toward a marvelous experience for its own sake. It had been leading her home.

Seeing her turn toward the road again, Kael asked quietly whether she needed to rest. Seren shook her head, but did not hurry them onward. She looked at both companions, tried to speak, and instead held out her hand. Elowen took it. After a moment's hesitation, Kael placed his hand over theirs. They stood together in the evening wind, making no attempt to force a single explanation upon what had happened. Each had seen and felt something different, and each still carried unanswered questions. Yet the road had made them more than three strangers who happened to be traveling in the same direction.

They descended the far side of the ridge, and when the last rise fell behind them, a great city opened before their eyes. High walls followed the riverbanks. Broad waterways ran through the districts within, and the crowded masts of ships rose above the harbor like a dark forest against the sky. Lights were appearing one by one in streets and on wharves. Bells sounded across the water and reached the travelers on the hillside. This was no longer a destination existing only in rumor. It was a living city of merchants and boatmen, nobles and guards, and countless ordinary families. Behind every light was a hope, a worry, a life continuing beyond the travelers' knowledge.

“Riverport,” Elowen said softly. She thought of the relatives she might seek out in the city, and of the responsibilities entrusted to her by the Order. Kael was already studying the approaches, looking for the roads to the ferry crossings and the places where guards might be posted. Seren watched the lights with the joy of arrival mingled with awe at everything still unknown. She did not know who might be waiting for her there, nor how the encounter on the ridge would shape the road ahead. She knew only that without all the seemingly insignificant choices behind her, she would not have arrived as the person she had become.

Riverport was settling into another evening. At the harbor, workers counted cargo; in houses across the city, families waited for loved ones who had not yet returned; and in brightly lit halls, powerful people weighed questions of influence and advantage. Strange reports from the north were beginning to travel along the roads and rivers, though few yet understood what they meant. To the city, the three figures descending the hillside were merely a handful among tomorrow's many arrivals. To the travelers themselves, one journey had ended, and new responsibilities were opening before them.

Seren looked back one last time. The ridge lay in the gathering dusk, and the wooded paths and stream where they had rested were hidden from sight. She remembered standing at a crossroads, afraid to take another step without an unmistakable sign. Now she no longer needed immediate proof for every choice.

She turned toward the lights. Kael adjusted his sword belt, Elowen lifted her pack, and together they followed the road toward the gates. Night settled behind them, but the light they had followed had not remained upon the ridge.
