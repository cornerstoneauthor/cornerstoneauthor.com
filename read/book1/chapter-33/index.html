CHAPTER 33

The Stranger

Three days after leaving the Great Road.

Aether and Kaelan arrived at a waystation built right beside the highway.

It wasn't large, but it hummed with activity. Travelers bound for distant lands stopped here to restock food and clean water, while merchant caravans pulled in to shelter for the night. To anyone braving long journeys, such a place felt like a quiet harbor in the middle of a vast wilderness.

By the time the two boys arrived, it was late afternoon.

Heavy freight wagons crowded the yard, draft horses tethered to wooden hitching posts. A few travelers sat gathered around wooden tables, drinking and talking low, while the air hung thick with the savory scent of roasting meat and woodsmoke.

Aether liked places like this. They were bound to be full of stories.

Just as they were looking for a spot to sit, a man approached them.

He looked to be in his mid-forties, clad in faded, threadbare clothes. Exhaustion was carved deep into his face, making him look years older than he likely was.

"Could you spare a drop of water?" the man asked, his voice rasping.

Aether didn't hesitate, handing over his waterskin immediately.

The man murmured his thanks, drank deeply, and passed the skin back.

A brief conversation revealed his name was Vic. He had been a merchant until a few months ago, when bandits ambushed his caravan along the road. He lost his cargo, lost his life savings, and his companions scattered. Now, he survived on odd jobs and the charity of strangers.

Aether listened intently, a stir of pity warming his chest.

When Vic spoke, it didn't sound like a story cooked up on the spot. The details were far too sharp. He could name the exact goods he’d carried, the road where the ambush took place, how many horses were slaughtered in the mud, and the exact coin lost. Things like that were hard to fake.

Kaelan, however, stayed dead quiet, simply listening from the side.

***

As dusk fell, Aether shared a portion of his hardtack with Vic.

Vic accepted it with deep gratitude, bowing his head repeatedly. "There are still good souls in this world," he murmured, sighing softly.

Aether offered a small smile. He felt that despite the man's horrific luck, Vic wasn't unpleasant company.

They stayed through the next morning to finish restocking their supplies.

During that time, Aether noticed that almost everyone around the waystation seemed to know Vic. Passing caravans dropped off bits of food for him; departing travelers tossed him a few copper coins; even the station master occasionally let him haul crates in exchange for a warm meal.

Everyone knew his tragedy, and everyone was willing to lend a hand.

Aether wasn't surprised. Had Vic wandered into Stony Creek, the villagers there would have done the exact same thing.

Then, around noon, Kaelan suddenly spoke up.

"His pack isn't light."

Aether blinked, confused. "What do you mean?"

Kaelan didn't answer right away. He simply glanced toward the corner where Vic’s travel pack rested.

It was a sizable bundle. Aether hadn't paid it much mind before, but thinking about it now—why would a man who claimed to have lost everything be carrying something so heavy?

***

That evening, while Vic was out helping a teamster unload freight, Kaelan walked over to the pack. He nudged it with his foot and gave it a slight lift.

It was heavy. Solid.

Aether frowned, a knot forming in his stomach. "We shouldn't be prying."

"I didn't open it," Kaelan replied plainly.

Aether didn't push further. A quiet doubt had begun to take root in his mind as well.

Night fell.

When Vic returned, he approached another traveler to ask for rations. His story was the same as before. "Things have been brutal lately... lost all my cargo... I have nothing left."

The traveler sighed, feeling sorry for him, and handed over a hunk of bread.

Aether felt an odd prickle of discomfort. That was the third time today Vic had asked for food.

Finally, a wagon driver who knew Vic well couldn't hold his tongue any longer. "Have you finished off everything in that pack of yours yet, Vic?"

Vic’s face paled slightly. The surrounding yard went dead quiet.

"What are you talking about?" Vic muttered.

The teamster let out a dry laugh. "The rations the merchant crew gave you last month. The copper coins the pilgrims handed you a few days back. The wool blanket the station master lent you. Are you telling us all of that just vanished into thin air?"

Vic fell silent, his jaw tightening.

Aether stared at him, stunned. For the first time, he realized Vic wasn't quite as destitute as he let on.

The station master finally shook his head and said bluntly, "You've got more supplies stuffed in that bag than half the travelers passing through here. Yet you still beg every single day."

Vic’s expression grew sour. He snapped back defensively, "I'm just saving it for later!"

"Later?" the teamster scoffed.

"Who knows what's going to happen down the road?" Vic spat. "I've lost too much already. A man has to keep a safety net for himself."

No one argued back immediately. What Vic said wasn't entirely wrong—a man who had lost everything had reason to be terrified.

Aether didn't know what to think. Vic *had* been robbed; he *had* lost his life’s work. Those facts were real.

Yet, why did hearing his defense leave such a bitter taste in his mouth?

***

Later that night, by the crackling campfire, Aether couldn't help asking Kaelan in a whisper, "Do you think he's a bad man?"

Kaelan shook his head. "No."

"Then why does everyone dislike him now?"

Kaelan thought for a moment before speaking softly. "Because he stopped."

Aether frowned, not quite understanding.

Before he could ask, an old, gray-haired traveler sitting nearby spoke up. He had clearly been listening to their hushed conversation.

"Talking about Vic?" the old man asked.

Aether nodded.

The old man tossed a dry branch into the flames. Silence stretched for a beat before he said, "The fellow isn't a villain. He isn't a crook, either. Truth be told, he deserves pity."

Aether nodded. That was exactly how he felt.

"The trouble is," the old man continued, his eyes reflecting the firelight, "he’s been sitting right here for over a month."

Aether froze. *A month?*

The old traveler nodded grimly. "When he first dragged himself in here, everyone helped him. Heck, people still help him now. But he’s never actually tried to leave."

The firelight danced across the old man’s weathered face.

"Getting knocked down by bad luck isn't his fault," the old man said softly. "But staying stuck here? That's his choice."

The air around the hearth went quiet.

Aether stared blankly into the flames, those words echoing over and over in his head.

***

Late into the night, long after most travelers had turned in, Aether remained awake.

He looked toward the entrance of the waystation. Vic was still sitting by the door, recounting his tragedy to a late-arriving traveler, still receiving pity, still anchored to the exact same spot.

Meanwhile, out on the Great Road, other travelers kept moving through the moonlight.

Some walked with a limp; some carried crushing loads; some had lost their families, and others had lost their livelihoods.

Yet they kept pushing forward.

Aether watched them in silence, understanding something fundamental for the very first time.

The difference between people didn't lie in what terrible things happened to them.

It lay in what they chose to do *after*.

Far off in the distance, the Great Road stretched eastward beneath the moonlight.

And Whitestone City drew closer with every step.
