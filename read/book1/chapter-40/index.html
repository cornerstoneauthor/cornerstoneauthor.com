CHAPTER 40

The Distant Wall

At the break of dawn, the caravan hauled itself back into motion.

The tide of displaced folk joined them on the high road, merging into a single, restless artery. What was once a dirt track had swelled into a raucous, churning river of iron-shod wheels, lowing beasts, haggard wanderers, and armored merchants—all drawn inexorably toward a single point.

Whitestone City.

Aether could no longer count how many times he had heard that name dragged through the dirt over the past weeks. The traders weighed it in silver; the refugees whispered it like a prayer; the garrison soldiers cursed it under their breath. It felt as if every track, river, and mountain pass across the realm had been carved with the sole purpose of bleeding into its gates. Yet until now, it had remained a ghost story—a legend perched far beyond the horizon.

By mid-morning, the road widened aggressively. Deep, crisscrossing wheel ruts had ground the earth so wide that four or five heavy freight wagons could roll shoulder-to- shoulder without clipping hubs.

The signs of civilization thickened along the verges. Roadside inns, vaulted grain silos, timber supply depots, and fortified military redoubts flickered past with relentless frequency.

"Is the whole world built like this?" Aether asked, leaning out over the carriage box to watch a mounted patrol ride past.

The guard beside him shook his head, spitting a red lump of tobacco leaf into the dust. "Not the world, kid. Just the guts of a real city."

Aether nodded slowly, a restless ache building in his chest.

Around noon, the road began to climb. It was no sharp mountain pass, but a long, agonizingly slow incline that drained the marrow from the draft beasts' legs. Thousands of souls shuffled up the grade, their boots heavy with pale grit.

Then, at the crest of the ridge, a sharp cry shattered the rhythmic crunch of the road.

"There!" a voice bellowed from the front of the line.

The cry caught like wildfire, leaping from cart to cart.

"Whitestone!"

"Look at it! We made it!"

Children scrambled onto the tops of water casks; weary women dragged themselves up onto wagon sideboards, their hands trembling. The smothering apathy that had hung over the refugees burst like an infected wound, replaced by a sudden, jagged surge of noise.

Aether’s heart hammered against his ribs. He vaulted onto the sideboard, his eyes sweeping the crest ahead. Kaelan lifted his head, his sharp eyes narrowing as both boys pushed through the throng toward the high ground.

Aether reached the summit and stared out into the distance.

At first, he saw nothing but haze. On the edge of the sky, where the earth met the pale sun, ran a long, dull grey line. It looked paper-thin, shimmering through the heat ripples rising off the plain.

"What is that?" Aether breathed. "A ridge? A mountain range?"

A veteran guard nearby let out a dry, gravelly laugh. "That’s her, lad. That’s Whitestone."

Aether froze, his breath catching tight.

He forced his eyes to focus. That gray ribbon wasn't a formation of rock, nor was it a bank of cloud. It was a wall—a monolithic, unbroken sheet of carved stone spanning the horizon, so vast that even miles away, it dwarfed the surrounding hills.

He had seen the wooden palisades of Stony Creek, the field outposts of the garrison, and the low dry-stone breastworks of Riverdale. But this was something alien. It sat across the earth like a sleeping titan, cold and indomitable.

"That's... really a wall?" Aether murmured, unable to strip the disbelief from his voice.

"You'll see when we get close," the guard muttered. "If your knees don't give out first."

The column lurched forward again, buoyed by a fresh wave of desperate energy. Aether had assumed that once the wall came into view, the city was practically beneath their boots.

An hour bled away. The grey line grew no larger.

Two hours passed. The wall remained a distant, immovable shadow.

By late afternoon, the sun dipped low, throwing long, bloody streaks across the sky. Only then did the distant silhouette begin to take on terrifying detail. The wall rose higher, revealing jagged crenellations and towering siege bastions jutting out every few hundred paces. Down at its base, the chaotic ant-like crawling of human crowds became visible.

And yet, the distance between them refused to close.

"Will we make it through the gates today?" Aether asked, rubbing his aching thighs.

The guard burst into a booming laugh. "Through the gates? Boy, if our boots touch the outer ditch before dark, we’ll be lucky."

Aether stared, baffled. How could something so clearly visible remain so impossibly far away?

Kaelan said nothing. His jaw was set, his dark eyes quietly recording every detail as the landscape shifted around them.

The virgin wilderness had completely vanished. In its place, a sprawling labyrinth of outer settlements clawed at the outskirts: massive timber barns, iron-smithy yards, livery stables, chaotic open-air markets, and fortified garrison checkpoints. Thousands of people lived, traded, and died out here without ever setting foot inside the inner stone ring.

Kaelan noted the sharp, iron edge of order that governed this chaos. Mounted patrols in polished kettle helms swept the verges every ten minutes. Laborers swept the gravel tracks. Tax clerks weighed wagons on massive iron scales, while armed bailiffs sorted the incoming tides into strict queues.

It took unimaginable coin and blood to keep a machine this large turning. Looking up at the monolithic ramparts looming ahead, Kaelan wondered for the first time just how many souls were crammed behind that stone.

By twilight, the sheer scale of Whitestone City crashed down upon them.

Aether realized with a chill that the stretch of wall he had spotted at noon was merely a single flank. Up close, the grey stone wall rose thirty feet into the air, blocking out the fading light like an artificial cliff.

At the center stood the grand gatehouse—an iron-reinforced archway wide enough to swallow a river of iron and wood. Thousands stood jammed before it: trading guilds, sellswords, refugees, farmers, and noble retinues, all waiting for clearance.

Just as their caravan drew near the outer ditch, a deep, bronze horn sounded from the battlements.

The brass note vibrated through the earth, heavy and mournful. High above, massive iron- bound oak doors began to swing shut with a grinding groan that sounded like breaking bone.

A collective groan rippled through the miles-long queue. Men spat in the dirt; women pulled their cloaks tight; drivers cursed and began hauling their teams off the main track.

Aether blinked in shock. "They're shutting it?"

"Gates close at dusk, kid," a merchant said casually, hopping down from his perch. "No exceptions. Not for the King himself."

"So what do we do now?"

"We sleep in the dirt and wait for dawn."

The merchant turned his team onto the flat hardpack outside the wall, his movements practiced and routine. Within an hour, the plain surrounding the city transformed into a sea of flickering campfires.

For the refugees, however, there was no disappointment—only an overwhelming, tearful relief. They had reached the wall. Families wept openly, falling to their knees in the dust to pray. Children ran between the fires, their exhausted faces lit with wonder as they pointed up at the massive battlements above.

Night swallowed the plain. Aether sat wrapped in his wool blanket, staring up at the titanic fortress.

High up on the battlements, torch after torch flared to life, casting a golden crown along the rim of the stone. The rhythmic glint of speartips marked the pacing of sentries moving along the high walk. In the dark, the city felt less like a collection of buildings and more like a great, iron-ribbed beast crouching over the world, promising safety to those beneath its shadow.

Aether watched until the fires blurred in his eyes, then finally pulled the blanket over his shoulders and lay back against the earth.

Through the gaps between the parked wagons, the immense grey stone wall still towered against the star-pocked sky. Far off, a sentry's horn blew one last time, its call swallowed instantly by the cold night wind.

Tomorrow, the gates would open. Tomorrow, they would step inside.
