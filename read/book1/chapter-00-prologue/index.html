BOOK ONE

THE LAST ECHO

The world remembers kingdoms. But every kingdom begins with a single forgotten voice.

The wind wept through the ruins.

It slipped between fractured pillars, brushed over collapsed domes, and raised a low, hollow moan through countless shattered colonnades. It felt as though an era long forgotten was still there, trapped in the deep belly of the stone, whispering its final secrets.

The sun was drowning now, bleeding its crimson light across the world.

At the edge of the desolation lay the carcass of a great, ancient city. Its spires had toppled. Its ramparts were broken. Structures once built to withstand eternity were now buried, choked beneath wild thorns and climbing ivy.

It had belonged to an age of majesty. But today, not a soul remembered its name. The world had forgotten it—just as the world had forgotten so many things.

Hope, among them. And the Echoes, too.

At the highest precipice of the broken tower sat an old man.

His white hair whipped around him in the gale. The lines carved into his face were deep and sharp, like fissures in a drought-cracked earth. He looked fragile, his body so withered that a strong gust might scatter him into dust. Yet his eyes remained fixed on the horizon.

He was watching the tree.

It was a colossal thing, a World-Tree that once seemed to bind the heavens to the earth. Even across leagues of barren wasteland, its silhouette loomed stark against the sky.

But it had died an age ago. It bore no leaves. It held no life. Only a pale, bleached skeleton of a trunk pierced the heavens, like the ribcage of a leviathan abandoned at the end of the world.

The old man stared at it. For hours. For lifetimes.

Then, he let out a soft, ragged sigh. “Another day gone.”

The wind offered no answer. Only the raven perched on his shoulder stirred. The bird had kept him company for many years—perhaps decades, perhaps centuries. The old man’s own memory had long since frayed at the edges.

The raven tilted its head, its crimson eyes gleaming like hot coals in the gathering dusk.

The old man smiled faintly. “Tell me,” he murmured. “Will today be any different?”

The raven did not croak. It did not move.

But at that exact moment, a thought clawed its way into the old man’s mind. It was cold, deep, and scraped against his consciousness like something dragging itself out of an abyss.

“No.”

The old man’s expression did not waver. He was too familiar with this internal intruder. “Is that so?”

The voice scraped again.

“Three hundred years. And still you watch. What is it you wait for, old man?”

The keeper of the tower looked toward the bleeding sky, remaining silent.

The voice pressed harder, dripping with ancient malice.

“They are all dead. The Kings have rotted. The Watchers are dust. The Empire is nothing but ash. Every soul who ever caught the Echo has vanished from this earth. Only you remain.”

The old man gently closed his eyes, letting the wind comb through his silver hair. “I know.”

“Then why not yield? Why not let go?”

A long, heavy silence stretched between them before the old man finally spoke.

“Because I heard it.”

The wind suddenly died. It was as if the entire expanse of the ruins had held its breath to listen.

The old man looked out into the vastness, his eyes growing distant, filled with the reflection of a time gone by.

“I heard the voice,” he whispered. “I heard it roll across the mountains. I heard it pierce the great rivers. I heard it echo through the hearts of men. It was a sound that made the broken stand tall again. It brought sight to those trapped in the dark. It guided the lost back home. I did hear it.”

The raven stared at him, unblinking.

The voice in his mind sneered. —Memory is a liar.

The old man shook his head. “Not this one.”

“Perhaps it was nothing but your own madness. An illusion.”

“It was real.”

” “Perhaps there never was an Echo.

“There was.”

“Perhaps you have wasted an entire lifetime waiting for something that never existed.”

The old man smiled. It was a weary smile, but entirely at peace.

“If it never existed,” he whispered, “then why are you so desperate to make me forget it?”

For the first time in three centuries, the voice went utterly silent.

Night fell like a heavy velvet shroud. One by one, the stars pierced the dark, and an infinite silence swallowed the wasteland. The old man sat there, frozen in time, looking for all the world like a primordial monument that refused to fall.

Hours bled into hours.

Then, abruptly, the wind shifted.

It was a change so subtle, so microscopic, that any ordinary mortal would have missed it. But the old man’s eyes snapped open.

He had heard something.

It was faint. Immeasurably distant. Like the first rustle of spring leaves, like the sea kissing a forgotten shore, like a mother whispering a name into a dreaming child’s ear.

The old man’s frame began to tremble. On his shoulder, the raven froze, its crimson eyes widening with a sudden, primal terror.

“No, the voice panicked in his mind. —It is impossible.”

The old man ignored the demon in his head. The sound was growing clearer now. It was gaining weight, gaining truth, as if it had traveled through thousands of years and crossed the span of a broken world to finally arrive here. To this forgotten ruin. To this old man who had spent his entire life waiting.

A single tear tracked down his weathered cheek.

The old man looked to the East.

At the very edge of the world, a microscopic speck of golden light flickered in the deep dark. It was like a newborn morning star. Like a spark that had slept beneath the ash for a millennium. Like the very first stroke of ink upon a blank page.

The raven let out a sharp, piercing shriek. It unfurled its pitch-black wings for the first time, desperate to flee.

But the old man was already standing up. His eyes were no longer clouded with age; the weariness had vanished. A fire, long thought extinguished, ignited within his pupils.

He gazed at the distant light, tears streaming down his face. After a long, breathless moment, he let out a soft, triumphant laugh—a laugh of absolute clarity, like a man who had finally solved the riddle of his existence.

“So...” he whispered.

The wind surged through the ruins once more, and the stars above burned with a fierce, renewed brilliance.

The old man’s voice was as steady as the earth beneath him.

“So... the lineage never broke.”

The voice in his head vanished entirely. The raven went still.

And in the East, the first true dawn began to tear through the ancient dark.

A new age had begun.
