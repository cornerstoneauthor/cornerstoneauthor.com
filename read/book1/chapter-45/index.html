CHAPTER 45

The Same Question

Whitestone was already at work not long after dawn. The doors of the Lord's Manor stood open while clerks crossed the corridors with bundles of records and messengers came and went almost without pause. In Lord Rayne's office, reports covered the desk: refugee placements, grain reserves, trade routes, patrol records. None of them seemed large by itself. Together they left little empty space.

Rayne sat behind the desk, reading a patrol report. Theft had risen over the past two weeks. So had fights in taverns and quarrels over work. Small things, most of them, but they were accumulating.

A knock came at the door.

"Come in."

A clerk entered with a sealed letter. "Urgent, my lord. From the south."

Rayne took it. The moment he saw the seal, his hand paused. He broke it, read the first lines, and fell silent.

"My lord?"

Rayne set the letter on the desk. "Riverport."

The clerk waited.

"It is moving faster than I expected." Rayne looked once more at the signature at the bottom of the page. "Find Edwin. Now. Tell him I need his help."

When the clerk had gone, Rayne read the letter again. Relations between Riverport and Ironbridge had worsened. Caravans had been stopped. Goods had been held. Militia were beginning to gather on both sides. No battle had begun, but the distance between dispute and violence was growing thin.

Thirty years ago, Rayne had believed those kinds of problems had been settled.

Perhaps they had only gone quiet.

Across the city, Edwin was drinking tea at his table. Kaelan was cleaning his short blade, while Aether watched people pass outside. Since Whitestone had agreed to send help toward Stony Creek, some of the tension had left Kaelan's face.

A sharp knock sounded at the door.

A messenger from the Lord's Manor entered and bowed. "Lord Rayne asks to see you."

"When?" Edwin asked.

"Now."

Edwin rose and reached for his coat.

Aether turned from the window. "Why does the lord want you?"

"Because he has something he wants done."

"What?"

"We'll know when we get there."

Aether frowned. Then another question occurred to him. "You know him well?"

"Well enough."

"As long as you've known Harold?"

A faint smile touched Edwin's face. "Near enough."

Kaelan looked up. "My father knew Lord Rayne?"

"He did."

"He never said so."

Edwin was quiet for a moment. "There wasn't much from those days worth boasting about."

That only made Aether more curious. "What did you do?"

Edwin stopped at the door. "Nearly got ourselves killed in Riverport."

Neither boy spoke.

"Come on," Edwin said. "If Rayne is calling me in, you may as well hear it too."

The closer they came to the center of Whitestone, the broader the streets became. Yet the Lord's Manor was not the place of polished ease Aether had imagined. Clerks hurried through crowded corridors. Messengers carried orders from room to room. Unread documents waited in stacks against the walls.

For the first time, Aether understood that governing a city did not look much like sitting above everyone and giving commands. It looked like facing one problem after another and deciding which one could not wait.

Rayne looked up when Edwin entered.

"You came."

"You sent for me."

"I thought you'd take longer."

"Then you underestimated me."

"I've always overestimated you."

For a moment, both men smiled. Aether noticed at once that this was not the greeting of a lord and an old soldier. It was the greeting of two men who had known one another for years.

Rayne motioned them to sit, then looked at the boys. "These are the two from Stony Creek?"

"You already know that," Edwin said. "Kaelan. Aether."

Rayne nodded. "Tell me what happened there."

Kaelan straightened and began. He described the attacks, the wounded villagers, the animals behaving strangely, and the growing fear that another assault would come. Rayne listened without interruption, asking only a few precise questions.

When Kaelan finished, Rayne folded his hands.

"I'll send people north. A patrol first. If what they find calls for more, we'll arrange more."

Kaelan stared at him. "You will?"

"I will."

The breath Kaelan released seemed to have been held since the day they left Gray Vale.

Rayne let the moment pass, then picked up the letter from his desk. "Now the other matter."

Edwin read it, and his expression tightened. "Riverport?"

"Yes."

"It's already this bad?"

"Worse than the letter makes it sound. Both sides are gathering militia."

Aether listened, not fully understanding the politics but understanding the weight in the room. "Why are they fighting?"

Rayne glanced at him. "Grain. Trade roads. Too many pressures arriving at once."

"Were they enemies before?"

"No. They worked together well enough."

Aether frowned. "Then why now?"

Rayne was silent.

"Because people protect their own first," he said at last.

"Even if they were friends?"

No one answered immediately. Rayne looked down at the letter and let out a quiet breath. Some questions were harder than moving grain or posting guards.

By the time the meeting ended, evening had begun to settle over the city. Aether and Kaelan were led into the corridor while Rayne and Edwin remained behind.

Rayne watched the door close. "Is the boy like Harold?"

Edwin shook his head. "No. Harold always wanted to solve the problem in front of him. Aether keeps asking why the problem is there at all."

Rayne looked at the reports on his desk - refugees, grain, trade, Riverport.

"Maybe we've spent too many years solving what comes after," he said.

Not long afterward, a clerk hurried down the corridor after Aether and Kaelan.

"Please wait. Lord Rayne wants you back."

When they returned, more reports had been spread across the table, along with a large map.

"There are a few things we did not finish," Rayne said.

"About Riverport?" Aether asked.

"About more than Riverport."

He pushed several documents toward them. Northern refugee numbers were rising. Trade along some roads was falling. Grain prices were climbing. Theft and fights were becoming more common. Relations between cities were fraying.

Kaelan studied them. "What do these have to do with one another?"

Rayne looked to Edwin.

Edwin arranged the papers in a line.

"Alone? Maybe nothing. Together..."

He touched the first. "More people leaving the north."

The next. "Less trade moving through the roads."

Then the others. "Higher food prices. Fewer jobs. More trouble in the streets. And now cities turning against one another."

Kaelan looked along the row. "Like a rope."

Edwin nodded. "The question is what is pulling the other end."

Rayne leaned back. "For years I've dealt with each thing as it came. Refugees arrive, we find somewhere to put them. Grain rises, we try to steady the price. A road becomes unsafe, we work around it. Two cities quarrel, we try to keep them talking. But there are more problems every year. Close one gap, another opens."

Aether listened, thinking of Gray Vale, Stony Creek, the families on the road, and the maps spread across Edwin's table.

"What if they're all coming from the same problem?" he asked.

Everyone looked at him.

Aether hesitated, then continued. "Where did it start first?"

The room became still.

It was a simple question. So simple that no one had asked it that way.

Rayne finally said, "Riverport."

"Why?"

His gaze shifted toward the dark window. "Because thirty years ago, something similar happened there. I wasn't lord then. Edwin hadn't retired. Harold was there too."

Aether leaned forward. "What happened?"

Rayne gave him a tired smile. "Another time. We believed we dealt with it."

"Now I'm not so sure," Edwin said.

Before anyone could continue, another report arrived.

Rayne read it and his face changed.

"More displaced families entered Whitestone today."

"From the north?" Edwin asked.

The clerk shook his head. "South."

That single word altered the room.

Rayne set the report down. "I want to know what is happening."

He looked at Edwin. "Will you go and see?"

Edwin's eyes moved to the map, to the place marked Riverport. Whatever waited there belonged not only to the present, but to memories he had spent years leaving alone.

At last he nodded. "I'll go."

"Ask for whatever you need."

The meeting seemed finished. Then Aether spoke.

"I'm going too."

Rayne looked at him in surprise. "Why?"

"Because I want to know how this began. If we never find the cause, we'll keep dealing with whatever comes after it."

Kaelan spoke next. "I'm going too."

"And your reason?" Rayne asked.

"Stony Creek. This time someone is going to help them. But if the cause is still there, there will be another Stony Creek. Then another. One day there won't be enough people to help them all."

His voice was quiet, but there was no uncertainty in it.

Rayne looked at Edwin. The choice belonged to the man who would have to travel with them.

Edwin studied both boys. For a moment he seemed to see someone else standing there - Harold, many years younger, deciding to step forward because no one had ordered him to.

"Do you know what you're agreeing to?" Edwin asked.

Aether shook his head. "No."

"But someone has to go," Kaelan said.

Rayne laughed softly, the first easy sound he had made all day. "Looks like you're not going alone."

Edwin sighed, but he did not object.

Late that night, the three left the Lord's Manor. Whitestone still shone with lamps and tavern light. Patrols moved through the streets as if nothing had changed.

But something had.

Stony Creek would receive help. Ahead of them, another road had opened - south, toward Riverport, toward events from thirty years ago, and toward an answer none of them yet understood.

Aether stopped beside Edwin on the steps. "What are you thinking about?"

Edwin looked into the dark beyond the city.

"If all of this really is the same problem," he said, "then what we've seen may only be the beginning."
