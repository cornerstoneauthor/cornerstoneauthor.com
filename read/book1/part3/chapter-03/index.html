CHAPTER 3

The Maps of Riverport

← PreviousPart III ContentsNext →

The Patrol Office stood not far from the Civic Registry. Going north along a broad street of stone slabs, the shops thinned away, and the people in the road were no longer merchants and carters but patrol soldiers in leather armor and cavalrymen leading their warhorses. No guards barred the gate. The registration certificate the Civic Registry had issued them was proof enough of their purpose. A young soldier took the document and glanced at it. "Investigating the beast calamity?"

He nodded. "Wait a moment, please." And he hurried off into the courtyard.

Aether stood at the entrance and looked the Patrol Office over. It was nothing like the quiet of a great residence. The courtyard was in constant motion: men carried wooden crates from place to place; a horse was being reshod; and farther off, a patrol of cavalry newly returned to the city, their cloaks crusted with mud, were helping a wounded companion toward the infirmary. No one shouted. And yet the place never stopped for a single moment.

Before long, an officer of middle years came out to them. He was tall, and the leather armor on his shoulders was worn in many places; at one cuff you could still see the stitches where it had been mended. He came up to the three of them and held out his hand first, without any airs at all. "I am Darren. The patrols of this district are in my charge."

Aether took his hand. "We have been investigating the beast calamity for half a year. We have come all the way from Whitestone."

Darren nodded. "Come inside and talk."

The room was not large. Maps hung on its walls, thick files were stacked upon the desk, and the air smelled of parchment, ink, and leather. Aether unrolled the map they had compiled along the way and spread it out gently. Darren did not interrupt him. He only watched in silence, and as mark after mark of the map was laid open before him, his expression grew grave by degrees. Not until the last mark had come into view did he raise his head slowly. "All of this — you investigated it yourselves?"

"Yes," Aether said, nodding. "Some of these places we even visited twice."

Darren was silent for a while. Then he nodded, once, lightly. "Well done."

Something in Aether's chest loosened a little.

But in the next moment Darren stood up and walked to the wall. Another map hung there, a larger one. He took it down and spread it on the desk.

Aether stared. The map was far more detailed than their own. The roads, the rivers, the villages and towns for hundreds of li around Riverport were marked upon it almost without exception — and the map bore marks of its own, mark upon mark, just as theirs did. Only their number was far, far beyond anything on the map the three travelers had made.

"These places," Darren said calmly, "we know them all."

The room went quiet all at once. Aether looked at the map and could not speak for a long time. For half a year they had believed that they, at last, had found the pattern by which the beast calamity was spreading. Only in this moment did they discover that Riverport had known all of it before them.

His eyes stayed on the greater map. He stepped closer, until he stood before it, and only then did he see how fine the marks truly were. Here, a dot in red. There, a short line in black. Elsewhere, nothing but a small, unremarkable cross. "These marks…" Aether asked softly. "Are they all the same?"

Darren came to stand beside the map and shook his head. "They are not." He reached out and pointed to several of the red dots. "Here, a patrol found a great herd of beasts." His finger moved to one of the black lines. "Here, two caravans were attacked." Then to another place. "And here, we closed the road."

His finger moved slowly across the map. Every mark stood for a patrol, an attack, or an order that had had to be given. And Aether understood, little by little, that what Darren had been recording was not the beast calamity at all. It was the reality the Patrol Office faced, day after day.

Darren withdrew his hand and looked toward the map Aether had spread out. "Your map is very much like mine. Only…" He smiled a little. "We have not drawn the same thing."

Aether looked down at his own map, and understood suddenly what he meant. What he himself had recorded was where the beasts had appeared. What Darren had recorded was where, after the beasts appeared, the patrols had gone — and what they had lost there. The same stretch of land. The same beasts. And yet two maps that were nothing alike at all.

The silence held for a while. Then, at last, Aether asked the question he had carried in his heart all along the road. "And… afterward?"

Darren looked at the map on the wall and did not answer at once. After a moment he said, slowly, "Afterward, we added more patrols. We escorted the caravans. We closed the roads. We moved out the villages that could not be held in time."

His voice was very calm, as though he were telling them something that happened every day of the week.

Aether pressed on. "What I mean is… why has it come to this?"

This time Darren was silent for longer. He looked at the map on the wall, as though he were truly turning the question over in his mind. In the end, he shook his head, lightly. "I do not know."

He said it quite openly. "Or rather… I have never thought about it."

Aether was taken aback. Darren turned around and looked at him. "The duty of the Patrol Office is to bring more people back alive after the danger has come. As for why it comes—" He smiled. There was no helplessness in that smile, and no evasion either; it was simply a soldier's plainest answer. "That is not my charge."

The room fell quiet again. Darren reached out and pushed Aether's map gently back across the desk toward him. "All the same. You have spent half a year walking through all these places. I think, myself, that you should go on investigating."

Aether raised his head. "Where should we go?"

Darren did not answer at once. Instead he turned and walked to the wall, and from a row of wooden racks took down an old rolled map. He did not open it. He only patted it, lightly. "In Riverport, everyone has a map of his own. We of the Patrol Office watch the patrols. The Merchants' Guild watches the trade roads. The Order watches people."

He paused there, and his gaze came back to rest on Aether. "Perhaps what they can tell you will be more than what I can."

Aether said nothing. He only looked down at the two maps lying on the desk. For the first time, he was beginning to feel that what he had spent half a year investigating was not the beast calamity at all — only a single part of this world.

It was close to noon when they left the Patrol Office. Darren did not detain them further. Only as they parted did he say, "Go and look in at the Merchants' Guild. The roads they travel every day are more than ours. Perhaps you will find something new there."

Aether nodded. The three of them rolled up their map and left.

Going south along the great stone avenue, the street grew livelier by degrees. Freight wagons passed in an unbroken stream, gangs of porters pushed their wooden carts through the press of people, and the air was thick with the smell of timber and leather, spice and sea wind. The farther they went, the more people there were. Edwin, watching the caravans pass along both sides of the street, said softly, "It is busier here than the last time I came — busier by a good deal."

Kaelen made no answer. He only observed the world around him in silence. The people here walked fast. Every one of them seemed to have a purpose of his own, and no one was willing to stop without good reason.

Before long, a broad stone hall appeared at the end of the street, and through its doors people streamed without pause: merchants, caravan leaders, guards, and not a few men with weapons at their waists. Inside, the hall was loud with voices. Merchants crowded around the counters arguing prices of goods, while several clerks kept an unceasing record of caravans about to set out. Down one side of the hall, before a tall wooden wall, stood a gathering of bounty hunters. The wall was covered with the day's newest commissions.

Kael was standing there. His gaze moved slowly across parchment after parchment: caravan escorts, bandit hunts, long-distance guard work, and a few long-term commissions at prices that were far from low. A member of the guild's staff who plainly knew him came over with a smile. "Here to look again today?"

Kael gave a slight nod. "Mm."

The man leafed through a few commissions not yet posted. "Not many short runs today," he said, smiling.

Kael glanced through them and shook his head a little. "Then forget it."

The man did not press him. He only set the parchments back on the desk. "Plenty of long roads lately, though. They just come back slower and slower."

Kael did not answer.

Just then, the great door of the guild hall was pushed open again, and Kael glanced back by instinct. Three people had come in: an older traveler, with two young men beside him. Their clothes were not those of merchants, nor of people who lived long in the city. What truly caught Kael's eye was the roll of a map that one of the young men carried in his arms. The map was worn to an obvious degree — its edges curled, and in many places it bore the marks of constant handling. Kael's gaze stayed on that roll of map for a moment. Not because he knew them. Only because, in the hall of the Merchants' Guild, very few people came in carrying a map like that.

He drew his eyes back and thought no more of it, turning again to the bounty wall before him. There was still nothing today that suited him. He turned and left, and soon vanished into the crowds streaming in and out beyond the hall doors.

On the other side of the hall, Aether's party had noticed nothing. They walked straight to the registration counter deep within the hall. Behind it, an old man with white hair was bent over his ledgers. When he heard their footsteps, he said by habit, without raising his head, "Caravan registration is on the left. Cargo declarations go to the back hall. Escort commissions — look in the main hall."

"We did not come to register a caravan," Aether said gently.

Only then did the old man raise his head. His gaze crossed the three of them and came to rest at last on the roll of the map in Aether's arms, and a flicker of surprise passed through his eyes. "You gentlemen are… not merchants. And not here to take a commission either."

Edwin stepped forward and bowed slightly. "Forgive the intrusion. Captain Darren of the Patrol Office advised us to come, and we have a few things we would like to ask the guild."

The old man's eyes swept the three of them once more, and stopped at last on the map roll in Kaelen's hands. He smiled. "Darren? Ah — so it was him. That captain of ours delights in sending people on to somebody else."

Edwin smiled as well. "He said the guild travels more roads every day than the Patrol Office does."

The old man nodded lightly. "That much is true enough."

He rose and went to the tall wooden cabinet behind him, where dozens of map rolls stood in neat order. With practiced ease he drew one of them out and laid it on the counter. "Come, then. Look at this first."

In silence, Kaelen spread out the map he had carried all the way, and the old man slowly unrolled the guild's map beside it. Aether stood at the table's edge and said nothing. His eyes moved continually back and forth between the two maps. It was the same country around Riverport; it covered most of the places they had traveled in the past half year. And yet, on the guild's map, there was not one single mark for a beast. No patrol lines. No closed zones. What covered it, dense as stitching, was trade roads and trade roads alone: which roads could still be traveled, which bridges had been closed, which ferries had ceased to cross, which caravans were forced to go by another way.

The old man stretched out his hand and let it move slowly across the map. "The Patrol Office concerns itself with where the danger is. What we concern ourselves with is which roads can still be walked." As he spoke, he brought out several more rolls of old maps and opened them one by one. "This is last year. This is half a year ago. This is now. When a road can no longer be walked, we change to another. When the next caravan comes back, it tells us again where a road can still be walked."

Edwin listened with care, and nodded slowly. "So what you have been recording all along is the trade roads — not the herds of beasts."

The old man smiled. "The guild deals with roads every day of its life. As for why the beasts are where they are…" He shrugged, lightly. "That is not a thing for us to worry over."

Kaelen had already, in silence, taken up a charcoal pencil, and had begun to add several of the new detour routes onto his own map. Through the whole of it, Aether had not spoken once. His gaze rested on the two maps, and for a long time it did not leave them. Only when the old man was about to roll the maps up again did Aether suddenly ask, in a low voice, "If the Patrol Office records the danger… and the guild records the roads… then has anyone ever…" He paused, looking at the two maps on the table. "…set them side by side, and looked at them together?"

The old man's hands stopped. Edwin turned his head to look at Aether as well. The old man was silent for a moment. In the end, he shook his head, lightly. "At least… I have never seen it done."

He rolled the map up again, but he did not put it back into the cabinet at once. He pondered for a while, as though turning something over, and then looked back at Edwin. "If you truly mean to follow these trade roads and keep investigating, then you had better not look at maps only."

Edwin inclined his head a little. "What do you mean?"

The old man raised his hand and pointed out of the window. Through the tall arched windows of the guild hall, the river harbor could be seen far off: mast upon mast standing against the sky, and ships without number moored along the wharves. "Every one of these roads, in the end, comes down to that place. The trade roads end on land. And there, they begin again."

He went on. "The guild knows which roads can still be walked. The Harbor Records Office knows where every cargo of goods has come from, and where it is meant to go. What things grow fewer and fewer. What things grow more and more. Which shipping lanes grow busier, and which lanes have had no ship back for a very long time." He smiled. "The people there keep books better than any of us. If something truly is changing, they will most likely have noticed it earlier than anyone."

Edwin bowed slightly. "Thank you."

The old man waved a hand. "No need to thank me. We each simply keep the records we are meant to keep. As for whether they can be joined together…" He glanced at the two maps on the table, and smiled a little. "That is beyond my gifts."

The three of them gathered up their map and left the Merchants' Guild.
