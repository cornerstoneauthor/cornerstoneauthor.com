CHAPTER 5

The Meeting

← PreviousPart III ContentsNext →

Edwin explained their errand once more. "We have followed the beast calamity all the way to Riverport. The Patrol Office told us where the danger lies. The Merchants' Guild told us which roads can still be traveled. The harbor records told us where the goods come from. But we were told that if we want to know how the people of these lands are truly living now, we should come to the Order." The brothers glanced at one another. One of the younger ones laughed first. "Then you have truly come to the right place. Though we have no maps here." The brother beside him shook his head, smiling as well. "And no ledgers, either. What we see on the road, we keep in our hearts." Everyone smiled, and the air in the yard eased all at once.

The elderly brother spoke slowly. "I look after a few mountain villages in the north. Since this winter, many of the young people have left. Most of those who remain are old." Edwin asked gently, "Why did they go?" The old man shook his head slightly. "Some say to make a living. Some say there is less and less game in the mountains. And some say only that they no longer dare to stay. I did not press them. Everyone has his own troubles, after all."

Another brother went on, "I go east of the river. A good many new families have come there, in fact — most of them moved in from somewhere else." Kaelen bowed his head and quietly marked two words down beside the map: east of the river.

A third brother smiled. "I go to the villages at the forest's edge." Slowly, his smile faded. "In the evenings, you used to see the children playing outside. Now the sun has not even set before every house has its doors shut, and hardly anyone comes out after dark." The yard gradually fell quiet.

At last, the middle-aged brother who had not yet spoken said softly, "I carry medicine to a few villages far off the roads. Once a year used to be enough. Now I have to make the trip two or three times a month." Edwin looked up. "Are there more sick people than before?" The brother shook his head. "Not many more, no." He paused for a while, then said quietly, "It is only that people are more and more afraid. They hope someone will come and see them — sit with them a while, talk with them — and tell them they have not been forgotten."

A light wind crossed the yard, and the leaves whispered faintly overhead. Aether had not interrupted a single one of them. Only when all the brothers had finished did he slowly lift his head and look at them. "You went to different places," he said softly. The brothers nodded gently. "But what you describe," Aether went on, "sounds as though you were all speaking of one and the same thing." The yard suddenly went still. The brothers exchanged glances, as though for the first time they were setting everything they had seen on the road side by side. A long while passed before the elderly brother let out a soft sigh. "Once..." He looked out toward the street beyond the yard, which was slowly coming back to life, and his voice was very low. "It was not like this, once."

At that moment, footsteps sounded outside the gate. The gate swung slowly open, and Elowen, Seren, and Kael walked in together. The young brother who had greeted the first visitors went to meet them, smiling. "Elowen. It has been a good while." Elowen bowed slightly, a smile on her face as well. "We were passing through Riverport, and I wanted to come and see everyone. I did not expect the house to be so lively today." The young brother laughed. "Several of our brothers happen to have come back today. It is not often we are all gathered together." As he spoke, he stepped aside and let the three of them into the yard.

And it was at that moment that Seren's steps suddenly slowed. Her gaze passed over the brothers talking in the yard and came to rest, lightly, on the young man far across it — Aether. She did not know him. She had never seen him before. And yet, in the instant her eyes found him, something rose in her heart that was so light and so faint she could not have named it — as though a fallen leaf had brushed the surface of a still lake: no sound, only a fine ring of ripples spreading slowly outward. Seren started slightly herself, and, without thinking, looked at him once more.

Aether was watching the brothers, listening closely as they went on speaking of the villages. He noticed nothing of what had happened by the gate, and he did not know that a stranger's gaze was resting on him.

Kael, standing behind Seren, was quick to notice the change in her. He did not look toward Aether at once. He looked at Seren first — in all their time on the road together, he had seldom seen her stop so suddenly in mid-step. Following her gaze, his expression slowly darkened. Them again. The Merchants' Guild. The harbor. And now the Order. His memory had always been good. He had never truly faced them, but the older traveler, the two young men, and the map they always carried with them — he remembered all of it. Once was chance. Twice could be explained. A third time? For the first time, a thread of wariness stirred in Kael, the instinct of a man long used to keeping watch. What were these people investigating?

Elowen had already walked over to the brothers and exchanged a few words of greeting with one of the old men when she realized that Kael had not followed her. She turned back, a little puzzled. "Kael?" Kael drew his gaze back and walked over slowly, his voice as calm as ever. "Those three." He did not turn his head, only gestured faintly toward the other side of the yard. "I have seen them three times now, these past days." Elowen followed his eyes and saw only three strangers, one of the young men sitting quietly there, listening to the brothers speak — saying nothing, drawing no attention to himself. She did not understand. "Do you know them?" Kael shook his head slightly. "No." He paused, then added, "That is why I find it strange."

Just then, the elderly brother standing nearby, who had overheard their exchange, looked across the yard and smiled. "Ah — you mean those three travelers from Whitestone. They have been investigating the beast calamity for more than half a year now. This morning they came to us specially, to ask how the villages are faring." Elowen started slightly. "Investigating the beast calamity?" The old brother nodded. "Yes. They have been to the Patrol Office, and to the Guild and the Harbor Records Office besides. Today they came here." At that, Elowen's gaze fell once again on Aether and his companions — and curiosity slowly kindled in her eyes.

The elderly brother looked from one party to the other, then lowered his head and smiled. "A happy chance. Since you are all interested — then let us talk together." With that, he raised a hand toward the far side of the yard. "Master Edwin."

The brothers who had been talking all turned their heads at once. Edwin rose and came slowly over, with Aether and Kaelen following behind him. Smiling, the old brother said, "Let me make you known to one another. This is Elowen, lately come back to us from the Order of the Sacred Lamp, and these two are the friends who traveled with her." Then he turned his gaze the other way. "And these three have come from Whitestone. For the sake of the beast calamity, they have been on the roads for more than half a year now."

The two parties exchanged nods. No one studied anyone else too closely, and the courtesies did not linger.

Then the brother seemed to remember something, and said with a smile, "Elowen — have you not been asking why the grain deliveries have grown more and more frequent of late? Well. What these three have been investigating all along their road bears directly on that very question."

Elowen looked at Edwin in surprise. "You have… been investigating for half a year?"

Edwin nodded gently. "More than half a year, to be exact. We have come all the way from Whitestone to here. And the further we investigated, the more we felt that this matter was not as simple as we had imagined."

Elowen frowned slightly. "The calls for help reaching the Order have indeed grown more numerous than before. We had only supposed it was a matter of a few places."

Then Aether, who had not spoken at all, suddenly asked in a low voice, "He said just now that the grain deliveries have been growing more frequent. Is it like that in a great many places?"

Elowen did not answer at once. Instead she looked toward the brothers who had only just returned from the road. They glanced at one another, and the elderly brother let out a soft sigh. "Not one place. And not one year. These past two or three years, places like that have grown more and more."

The yard fell suddenly quiet, and no one spoke. For only in that moment did both parties truly understand: what they had each seen along their roads were not two matters at all, but two faces of one and the same thing.

The silence held a while, and in the end it was Elowen who spoke first. She looked at the maps on the table, worn pale with handling, and asked softly, "All this half year… this is what you have been investigating, all along?"

Edwin nodded. He did not hurry to explain. Instead, he gently unrolled the map the Patrol Office had drawn. "At first, it was a single village. Then it was several villages. Then, later, we found that similar changes had begun to appear in different places. This is the Patrol Office — they record where the beasts have appeared." Then he spread out the Merchants' Guild's map beside it. "This is the Guild — they record the trade roads that can still be traveled." And last, he laid down the pages copied from the harbor records. "And this is the harbor — they record where goods have come from, and where they are bound."

The brothers all gathered around, looking down at the things on the table in silence. Edwin went on, "Every one of these records is true. And not one of them is wrong. And yet—" He paused. "Not one of them can tell us why."

The yard was quiet again. Elowen's gaze rested on the three records, so different from one another, and for a long time it did not move away. Then she said, very softly, "So it is not that no one knows about the beast calamity. It is that no one knows why it grows worse and worse."

A smile of quiet approval touched Edwin's face. "That is exactly it. All along our road, everyone has done his own work with care. The Patrol Office protects the people. The Guild keeps the trade roads open. The harbor records the goods." He looked toward the brothers. "The Order cares for people. No one has failed in his duty. And yet what each of us sees is only the part that lies before his own eyes."

The elderly brother sighed softly. "That sounds very much like us. Each of us walks a few villages, and when we come home, seldom does anyone set what we have all seen side by side."

Aether slowly raised his head. It was the first time that day he had taken up the thread of the talk on his own, and his voice was as calm as ever. "If these are set side by side, it is not hard to see. They were, perhaps, one and the same thing from the beginning."

No one answered at once, for no one knew the answer. And yet in every heart there, for the first time, the question was being turned over in earnest.

Elowen watched the three travelers in silence. She had a very strange feeling: what these men were investigating was not the beast calamity at all. What they were investigating might be something far more serious than the beast calamity. Without willing it, she thought of something Odren had once said to her: "Many things — you must learn to see the ties between them." At the time, she had not truly understood. But now, looking at the three records on the table, she felt all at once that perhaps Odren, too, ought to hear what these people had seen along their road.

No one spoke again. In the yard there was only the sound of the wind moving through the leaves.

Seren stood quietly where she was, her eyes still resting on Aether. Or rather, she was not truly looking at him. Her gaze seemed to fall upon him, and yet to pass beyond him. The feeling was still there — so light, so faint — and it had not faded with the passing of time. Instead it spread slowly through her heart, ring upon ring, as a breeze spreads ripples across the surface of water. She could not have said what it was, nor why it should have come to her from a young man she had never met. She only knew that it was not the young man himself. It was as though… something had once rested there, and left behind it a mark so fine it could scarcely be perceived.

Seren's brow knit almost imperceptibly. She had never known such a feeling before. She had waited in quiet, time after time, for that familiar answer; in long silences she had learned how to discern. So she was quite sure: what she felt today was not the same as what had come before. It gave no guidance. It made no call. It held no clear words at all. It simply existed, quietly — like a seed buried long ago in the earth, which has not yet broken into sprout, and yet already carries the breath of life within it.

Without thinking, she looked toward Aether once more. He had bowed his head and was putting the maps away again, one by one. He was as earnest as he had been a moment before, and as calm, as though he had no idea that the words he had just spoken had set everyone in the yard to thinking. He did not even know that someone was watching him in silence.

Seren drew her gaze gently back. She told no one — for she herself would not have known how to begin. She only laid that unfamiliar feeling quietly away in her heart.

At that moment, Kael, standing behind her, let his gaze travel silently over every person in the yard. He did not look at Aether again. He looked at Kaelen. Just now, at the very moment Seren had halted in her steps, that young hunter had also — almost in the same instant — shifted half a step closer to Aether's side, without drawing any notice. The movement was as natural as a man idly choosing where to stand, but it could not escape eyes that were themselves long accustomed to watching everything around them. Kael's gaze sharpened almost imperceptibly. That was the instinct of a man protecting a companion.

Nearly in the same moment, Kaelen became aware of that gaze. He raised his head slowly, and across the space between the people, their eyes met lightly. There was no hostility in it. No probing. And no words at all. Each of them simply saw, in the other's eyes, something familiar: vigilance. In the next instant, the two of them looked away almost at the same time, as though nothing had happened at all.

At the center of the yard, Edwin was rolling up the last of the maps. He bowed slightly to the brothers. "Today, we thank you all. At the least, we know now that this matter is far more serious than we had imagined — and so the investigation must not stop."

The elderly brother nodded gently. "May the truth, in the end, travel farther than our own footsteps."

Elowen listened to those words in silence and did not speak again. She only watched the maps, which had been unrolled and rolled up so many times, and a thought rose slowly in her heart: what these people were seeking was no longer the beast calamity alone. They were seeking the truth that no one had ever been willing to set side by side and look at whole. And a thing like that… her uncle ought to know of it.
