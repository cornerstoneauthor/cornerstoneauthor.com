CHAPTER 6

Entrusted

← PreviousPart III ContentsNext →

The elder brother rose slowly to his feet and glanced at the sky above the courtyard. "Well," he said with a smile, "it seems lunch will be late again today." The other brothers laughed, and the heaviness that had hung over the yard eased a little. One of the younger brothers stood up. "I still have to deliver medicine to the west of the city." Another shouldered the medicine chest he had set down beside him. "I am going east of the river this afternoon. There are two old people waiting there."

They did not linger on the subject of the beast calamity. For the brothers, talking was only the beginning; what truly mattered was to go on walking the road. Edwin watched them and smiled faintly. "No wonder the harbor registrar sent us to you. What you know is not written on paper." The elder brother waved a hand. "What we know is only the people we have seen along the road. As for why things have come to this..." He shook his head gently. "I am afraid that is for you to keep searching out." Edwin nodded gravely. "We will go on searching. Whatever the answer turns out to be, at least someone has to draw these things together."

The brothers exchanged glances. No one said anything more; they only bowed silently to the three travelers. It was not a farewell. It was more like a charge, handed over without words.

Nearby, Elowen was also taking her leave of the brothers one by one. She turned to go, then paused and looked back toward the center of the yard, where Aether stood with his head bowed, carefully rolling the maps up again — handling them as though they were not sheets of parchment at all, but the traces left behind by every village and every person along the road they had traveled. She did not go over to him, and she said nothing more. She only kept the sight of it quietly in her heart.

Seren stood beside her. That faint, elusive feeling, as the distance slowly grew, settled into quiet as well. She lowered her head slightly. She did not chase after it, nor try to name it; she only accepted, in silence, that small response she still did not understand. Kael looked one last time at Aether and his companions, then at Kaelen. This time neither of them turned back, as if the brief clash in the courtyard had never happened. "Let us go," Kael said softly.

The three of them walked slowly out of the Order's house. Afternoon sunlight lay across the flagstones, and Riverport was as busy as when they had arrived: vendors still cried their wares along the street, and from the harbor in the distance came the long, slow tolling of a ship's bell. Nothing had changed. Yet Elowen was strangely quiet all the way. Matthew glanced at her in surprise. "Miss?" Elowen seemed to come back to herself. "Matthew," she said softly, "does my uncle have any engagements this afternoon?" Matthew thought for a moment. "His lordship meant to meet the envoys of several lords, but he should be back at the residence by evening." Elowen nodded slightly and was silent for a while before she went on, slowly, "Today... I met some people. I think my uncle should hear what they have found along their road of investigation."

Matthew did not press her with questions. He only kept the words quietly in his heart, for he knew that Elowen seldom offered an opinion on strangers, and still more seldom said that his lordship ought to know a thing. He sensed, dimly, that the conversation at the Order's house today might matter more than it appeared to on the surface.

The four of them said little more on the way; the streets were still crowded with people coming and going. Not until they had entered the residence and were about to part did Kael stay behind — he called out to Seren. "Seren." She lifted her head slightly. Kael looked ahead of him without turning back, his voice as calm as ever. "What happened, just now?" Seren was silent for a moment; she knew what he was asking. "I do not know," she said, shaking her head gently. "It is only..." She paused, as if searching for a word she herself could not be certain of. "On that young man, I sensed something." Kael turned his head at last. "What thing?" Seren was silent again, and for a long time. Then she said softly, "Not him. It is something that has remained upon him." She shook her head slowly. "I cannot name it, and I do not know what it means. But..." She looked toward the Order's house, already far behind them. "That feeling was real."

Kael did not ask any further. Another person, hearing such words, might have found them hard to understand — but he would not. He knew that Seren never spoke lightly of something she had sensed; if she said it, then it was true. She simply did not yet know what it was. Kael nodded slightly. "I understand." No doubt, no analysis, and no more questions. The two of them walked on, and the understanding built along their road together let the conversation end just there.

Meanwhile, Aether and his companions were also walking slowly out of the Order's house. Kaelen suddenly glanced back. Aether noticed it. "What is it?" Kaelen was silent for a moment before he answered slowly, "That young man who kept standing behind the two girls." Aether thought for a moment. "The one who looked like a guard?" Kaelen nodded slightly. "Yes. He is very good." Aether was a little surprised. "How could you tell?" Kaelen did not answer at once. He lowered his head, as if recalling every detail of what had just passed. "He does not say much, but from beginning to end he was watching. Watching the Order's house, watching us, watching everyone who came near." He paused. "And later, he began to watch me." Aether blinked. "You knew?" Kaelen smiled. "Because I was watching him, too."

The three of them walked on, and Kaelen's voice remained level. "He does not feel like an ordinary guard. Alert, steady, and..." He let out a soft breath. "There is a strong pressure about him. Not something he puts on deliberately — more like something left behind after a great deal of danger." Aether looked back at the Order's house, already far in the distance; Kael and the others were long out of sight. He smiled faintly. "I admire you more and more. I did not sense a single thing." Kaelen gave a low chuckle. "That is a hunter's instinct, and you are no hunter. But..." He paused, and turned toward the Order's house once more. "It seems Riverport has no shortage of capable people." He said no more after that, but in his heart he kept that man's face in memory, for a hunter's instinct told him they would meet again.

The sun was sinking slowly toward Riverport. Elowen came out of her room and saw Hobbes, the old butler, arranging the evening's affairs in the front hall. When he saw her, he bowed slightly. "Miss. His lordship sent word that he will be back late tonight — the city lord's residence has suddenly called several lords' envoys together to confer." Elowen nodded gently. "I see." She had meant to go straight back to her room, but she stopped, and after a moment's silence asked softly, "When my uncle comes back... would you let me know?" Hobbes raised his head slightly. Matthew had already told him; he had not expected Elowen to ask once more herself, that she would wait for Odren. He did not ask anything further. He only nodded. "Yes, miss."

Night came on gradually, and at last the lamp in the study was lit. Odren pushed the door open and came in, a trace of weariness on his face that he could not quite hide. He had only just taken off his cloak when Hobbes knocked gently at the door. "My lord." "Come in." Hobbes entered and set a newly delivered document on the desk, then said slowly, "The young lady has returned." Odren nodded. "How was her day?" Hobbes smiled. "She went to the Order." Odren picked up his quill, ready to work through the documents. "It seems she is slowly growing used to Riverport." But Hobbes did not leave. Odren raised his head and looked at him, and Hobbes went on, "After the young lady came back, she did not rest right away. She asked especially that, when you returned, she would like to see you." The hand holding the quill paused slightly. "Did she say what it was about?" "No." Hobbes shook his head gently. "But... today, at the Order, she met three travelers from Whitestone." Odren looked up, and for the first time truly stopped what he was doing. "Whitestone?" Hobbes nodded. "They say the three have been investigating the beast calamity for more than half a year now, all the way from Whitestone to Riverport. Today, at the Order, they talked for a long time with several brothers who travel out to preach."

Odren fell silent. He did not press for more. He only leaned back slowly in his chair, his gaze resting on the stack of documents on the desk that had not yet been opened. On top lay the Patrol Office's monthly report; beneath it, the harbor's statistics; below those, a petition from the Merchants' Guild; and there were several letters from the outlying lords as well. Every one of them spoke of something different, and not one of them pointed toward the same answer. Hobbes stood quietly to one side and said nothing. A long while passed before Odren gently closed the document in his hand. "Ask the young lady to come," he said. He paused, then added, "Everything else tonight — set it aside for now." Hobbes bowed slightly. "Yes, my lord."

Quiet settled over the study again. Odren looked at those documents on his desk, each one from a different place, lost in thought. This was the Riverport he faced every day — and the city he worked every day to piece together, yet had never been able to piece together whole.

Before long, there came another soft knock at the study door. "Uncle." "Come in." Elowen pushed the door open and entered, bowing slightly to Odren first. Odren set down his quill and gestured for her to sit. "Hobbes tells me you went to the Order today." Elowen nodded gently. "Yes. And there, I met three men from Whitestone who are investigating the beast calamity." Odren said nothing, only watched her quietly — he knew that if Elowen had come to him of her own accord, it would never be merely to introduce a few strangers. Sure enough, Elowen went on, "They have been investigating all along, for more than half a year, all the way from Whitestone to Riverport. The Patrol Office, the Guild, the harbor records, and the Order — they have been to all of them." Odren leaned back slightly in his chair. "Why?" Elowen knew he was not asking why they had come to Riverport, and so she answered, "Because they want to know why the beast calamity keeps growing worse." "Have they found the answer?" Elowen shook her head gently. "No. But they have discovered one thing." Odren did not hurry her; he only waited in silence. Slowly, Elowen said, "The Patrol Office has its records, so it knows where the danger lies. The Guild has its records, so it knows which roads can still be traveled. The harbor has records of where goods come from, so it knows where they have grown more and where they have grown less. The Order knows where some of the beast packs have appeared, and where people are gathering, and where people are leaving. What every one of them knows is true. But..." She paused. "There is not a single place that can draw all of these things together."

The study fell silent at once. Odren did not answer immediately. He lowered his head and looked at the documents on the desk — the Patrol Office, the harbor, the Guild, the lords. They arrived on his desk every day, and for all these years, it seemed, he too had only ever dealt with them one by one. Elowen went on, "Uncle, I do not know whether they will be able to find the answer in the end. But... I feel that what they are investigating is already more than the beast calamity." At last Odren raised his head. For the first time that night, he looked at Elowen with real attention — not because of the three travelers, but because of what she had just said. All at once he realized that this girl, who had grown up in the Order all her life, was beginning to learn to see, through the things themselves, the connections between them. After a long silence, Odren spoke slowly. "This is what you thought of today." Elowen shook her head gently. "No. It is only after following their investigation, all along their road, that I slowly came to think of it."
