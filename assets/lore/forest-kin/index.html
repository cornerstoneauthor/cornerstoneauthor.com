THE LORE OF ELARION

FOREST KIN

The forest does not contain only trees. It contains a world.

ALL THINGS WITHIN

A tree may appear to stand alone. It does not.

Its roots depend upon earth and water. Its branches reach toward light. Creatures shelter beneath it. Others feed upon its leaves and fruit. When it falls, what remains becomes shelter, soil, and nourishment for lives yet to come.

So it is throughout the forest. Rivers nourish roots. Roots hold earth. Plants sustain animals. Life feeds life. Death returns what was living to the ground from which other life will rise.

Nothing living exists alone.

THE CREATOR'S INTENTION

The Creator made all things. The Forest Kin begin there.

And nowhere, they believe, are so many parts of creation gathered together as completely as within the forest.

To the Forest Kin, this cannot be without meaning.

Among their oldest traditions is the belief that the forest preserves a particular intention of the Creator—something written not in words, but in the way creation lives together.

THE WAY WITHIN

The Forest Kin do not search beyond creation for the way back to its Maker. They believe the way remains within creation itself—and nowhere more clearly than within the forest.

To walk deeper beneath the trees is therefore more than a journey through wilderness. It is to enter more deeply into the order of creation.

Within the forest, there remains a way home.

THE SACRED FOREST

The Forest Kin hunt. They gather. They cut wood. They build homes and shape tools from what the forest provides.

They do not believe that honoring the forest means leaving everything within it untouched. Life itself does not live that way.

A tree may become timber. An animal may become food. A clearing may become a home. But the forest itself is more than the sum of what can be taken from it.

The forest is not sacred because nothing within it may be touched. It is sacred because everything within it belongs to something greater.

TO PROTECT THE WAY

To destroy an ancient forest is not, to the Forest Kin, simply to remove trees. It is to break apart something that generations of life have formed together.

What others may see as land or timber, the Forest Kin may see as part of the path by which creation still leads toward its Maker.

To protect the forest is to protect the way home.

LIFE, DEATH, AND RETURN

Death is everywhere in a forest. Leaves fall. Animals die. Ancient trees collapse. Yet what dies does not simply vanish.

The Forest Kin grieve death as deeply as any people. But they do not easily understand death as standing entirely outside the order of life.

To die is not always to leave.

THE PATIENCE OF TREES

A kingdom may rise and fall while an ancient tree still stands. The forest measures time differently.

Some things cannot be forced into being. Some wounds cannot be healed by command. Some answers become visible only after enough seasons have passed.

Not everything that is slow is standing still.

WHAT THE FOREST HOLDS

The Creator made all things.

The forest gathers all things.

And somewhere within it, the Forest Kin believe, the way home remains.
