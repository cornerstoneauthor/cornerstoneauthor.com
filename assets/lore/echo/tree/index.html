THE LORE OF ELARION

THE TREE

Before much of history was written, the Tree was already there. And it remains after much of that history has been forgotten.

A CREATED THING

The oldest traditions are clear on one point: The Tree is not the Creator.

It was made. It belongs to creation, as the rivers, mountains, stars, and every living thing belong to creation.

Yet those same traditions insist that its existence was not ordinary. Among all that was created, the Tree was entrusted with a particular purpose.

A created thing, entrusted with a purpose.

WHEN THE CREATOR WAS NEAR

The oldest stories about the Tree are strange not because of what they say about the Tree, but because of what they say about the world around it.

They speak of an age in which the Creator was not understood as distant. In those oldest memories, the Creator was not someone creation was trying to find. He was near. And the Tree stood within that nearness.

The oldest stories do not describe the Tree as a place where people went to search for the Creator. They describe a time when no such search was necessary.

WHAT HISTORY REMEMBERS

The stories surrounding the Tree are filled with things that later ages found difficult to explain: voices heard where no speaker could be found, memories older than those who carried them, answers that came without words, lives altered beneath its branches.

Centuries later, scholars argued over such stories. Some called them miracles. Some called them symbols. Some called them embellishments.

The records remain. Their meaning does not.

THE TREE OF ECHOES

One name appears again and again among records older than many of the kingdoms that later claimed the lands around it: The Tree of Echoes.

Why it was called this is no longer agreed upon. Some say the name came from voices. Others from memory. Others from the belief that nothing truly spoken beneath its branches was ever entirely lost.

What has passed does not always become silent there.

FROM WONDER TO HISTORY

Time did not erase the Tree. It changed the way people understood it.

What one age called wonder, another called legend. What one generation received as testimony, another studied as tradition. What had once pointed beyond itself became a symbol.

The Tree had entered history. Or so people believed.

HISTORY OR WITNESS?

Kings came before the Tree. They died. Kingdoms rose within reach of its memory. They vanished. Languages changed around it. Names changed. Beliefs changed. The Tree remained.

To the modern world, the Tree belongs to history. To the oldest traditions, history belongs before the Tree.

WHAT THE WORLD FORGOT

The world did not forget the Tree completely. Its image survived. Its name survived in fragments. Its stories entered chronicles, songs, ruins, rituals, and arguments.

People remembered that something had happened there. They simply became less certain what that something meant.

Perhaps the Tree was never truly forgotten. Perhaps only its meaning was.

IT REMAINS

The Tree has been called a wonder, a bridge, a memory, a holy place, a relic, a legend, a mystery.

But whatever names later ages gave it, the oldest traditions never placed the Tree above the One who made it. It was created. It was given a purpose.

A history. A record. A witness.

And perhaps, still—something more.
